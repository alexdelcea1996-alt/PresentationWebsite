I build static, custom sites. You are entitled to read what follows with that in mind — so let me
start with the part that does not suit me.

## When WordPress is the right answer

**When you publish often and want to do it yourself.** If you add articles, products or notices
several times a week, you need an admin panel you can use without asking anyone. WordPress was built
for that and does it well.

**When you need a complicated feature, cheaply.** Paid bookings, subscriptions, a forum, a membership
system. There are plugins that have existed for years for each. Built from scratch, those are weeks
of work.

**When you already have somebody who knows it.** A nephew, an employee, an agency you work with. A
system your people know beats a better system nobody knows.

**When the budget is small and something has to exist this month.** A bought theme, filled in, beats a
perfect site that never gets made.

If you recognise yourself in any of those four, you do not need me to tell you otherwise.

## What you are actually buying with each

**WordPress** is a program running on your server that builds each page at the moment somebody asks
for it. It has a database, an admin panel, plugins. All of that is both the advantage and the cost:
more parts doing more things, and more parts to keep current.

**A static site** is a stack of files written in advance. The server builds nothing, it just sends
them. There is no database, so there is nothing to break in through; there is no admin panel, so
either somebody else changes the content or it is wired to a separate editing system.

The most visible difference is speed, and not because one is "better programmed". It is because one
does work on every visit and the other did it once, at build time.

## The five questions that decide for you

**1. How often does the content change?**
Daily or weekly → WordPress, or a static site wired to an editing system. A few times a year → static,
without hesitation.

**2. Who makes the changes?**
You, alone, at 11pm → you need a panel. You message me and I make them → you do not.

**3. Do you need features that already exist as plugins?**
If your list is long and specific, WordPress saves you real money.

**4. How many visitors, and from where?**
Heavy mobile traffic, poor signal, people who will not wait → speed matters more than the panel.

**5. Who handles the updates?**
This is the question everybody skips. See below.

## The part nobody mentions during the sale

A WordPress site needs updating. The core, the theme and every plugin get new versions, and some of
those fix security problems. A site left un-updated for a year does not break on its own — but it
becomes the easiest target on the street.

That means somebody has to log in monthly, update, and check nothing broke. Either you or somebody
paid. It is a real cost and it belongs in the sums from the start. I wrote about it separately in
[what you pay monthly after launch](/en/blog/what-you-pay-monthly-after-launch/).

A static site has no mandatory updates because nothing runs on the server. That does not make it
superior — it makes it different on maintenance.

## Myths on both sides

**"WordPress is slow."** No, badly configured WordPress is slow. With good hosting, caching and few
plugins it can be fast. The trouble is that most installations are not, and the causes are usually the
same four as in [why your site loads slowly](/en/blog/why-your-site-is-slow/).

**"WordPress is insecure."** No, un-updated plugins are insecure. The core is maintained seriously.

**"A static site cannot be edited."** It can. You wire it to a separate editing system that looks like
a panel. It costs a little extra at build time.

**"A custom site is always better."** No. A badly made custom site is worse than a good theme, and it
costs more too.

## How I choose, briefly

Business site, small shop, campaign page, content that changes rarely: static. You gain speed, quiet
on security, and no obligatory monthly cost.

Daily publishing, a shop with hundreds of products and variants, community features: WordPress or a
dedicated platform, no regrets.
