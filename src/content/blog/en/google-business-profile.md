If you run a business people come to — or that your people travel out from — there is one free thing
that brings in more calls in the first year than the website does. Nobody sells it, because there is
no commission in it.

It is your Google Business Profile: the card that appears beside the results, and the listings that
appear on the map when somebody searches "barber near me".

## Why it matters more than it looks

When somebody searches for a local service on a phone, the first thing they see is not the ordinary
results. It is a map with three listings above them. Those three take a very large share of the calls
that come out of that search, and the sites underneath share what is left.

Getting into them does not require a fast site, a blog or a budget. It requires a complete profile,
reviews, and an address that matches reality.

## What to fill in, in the order that matters

**1. The primary category, chosen correctly.** This is the one field people get wrong and it changes
everything. Google matches the search against the category, not against your description.
"Restaurant" and "Pizzeria" are not the same thing to it. Pick the most specific category that
describes you exactly, then add secondary ones.

**2. The name, exactly as it really is.** Not "John's Barbers — the best barber in town". Stuffing
keywords into the name breaks the rules, and profiles get suspended for it.

**3. The opening hours, holidays included.** A profile saying "Open" while you are shut produces a
wasted journey and a one-star review. Special holiday hours are a separate field and are skipped
almost every time.

**4. The phone number and the website address.** The number you actually answer. If you have a page
dedicated to your main service, link that one, not the home page.

**5. Real photographs, taken by you.** Not stock. The inside, the outside from the street (so people
recognise the place when they arrive), the team, the work. Ten good photographs beat a hundred
mediocre ones.

**6. Services and products, with prices.** There is a separate list for these. Few businesses fill it
in, and it is exactly the information people are looking for.

**7. Questions and answers.** You are allowed to post the frequent questions yourself and answer them
yourself. It is permitted and it is useful: you answer once, for everybody.

## Reviews: the hard part, and the decisive one

There is no honest shortcut here. Bought reviews are visible, against the rules, and put the profile
at risk.

What works is simple:

- **Ask.** Most happy customers will leave a review if you ask and hand them the link. They do not do
  it spontaneously.
- **Ask at the right moment** — right after the job has gone well, not three weeks later.
- **Send the direct link.** The profile dashboard generates a short link that opens the review box. A
  QR code on the counter or the invoice does the rest.
- **Reply to all of them.** Briefly to the good ones. And above all to the bad ones, calmly and
  factually. A good reply to a two-star review convinces more than ten five-star ones.

A bad review you answered civilly is proof that you exist and that you are present. A profile with
nothing but five stars and no replies looks bought.

## The mistakes I see most

**The address does not match everywhere.** Name, address and phone number must be written identically
on the site, in the profile and in any directory you appear in. "St." in one place and "Street" in
another creates doubt.

**An unclaimed profile.** Plenty of businesses already have an automatically generated listing that
nobody controls. Search for your business on Google Maps; if it appears and says "Claim this
business", do it today.

**No posts at all.** The profile has a posts section. It does not need feeding daily, but a completely
dead profile reads like a closed business.

**No connection to the site.** If you have a page per service, link them from the profile. They feed
each other — see also [why nobody finds you on
Google](/en/blog/why-nobody-finds-you-on-google/).

## When it will not help you

If you sell exclusively online, nationwide, with no premises anybody visits, the profile matters far
less. Fill it in anyway — it takes an hour — but do not expect calls from it.
