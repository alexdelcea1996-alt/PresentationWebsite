Text is the number one reason websites run late. Not the code. I said as much in [what to prepare
before you order a website](/en/blog/before-you-order-a-website/), but there I only said *that* you
need it. This is *how*.

The good news: you do not need to be good at writing. You need a method.

## The method, in one sentence

**Write down the answers you already give on the phone.**

You explain what you do, who for, what it costs and how long it takes, dozens of times a month. You
are already good at it — otherwise you would have no customers. The problem is that the explanation
lives only in your head and gets spent one person at a time.

Concretely: next time a customer calls with questions, write them down. Not the answers — the
questions. After ten calls you have the outline of your website, written by your customers.

## The trick that beats the blank page

Do not write. **Talk.**

Open the voice recorder on your phone and answer out loud, as if somebody were in front of you: "Right,
so what one of those costs at my place is..." Talk for five minutes. Then transcribe it — the phone
will do that for you — and cut what is surplus.

The text that comes out is better than what you would have written, because it is the language you
use with customers. Sites that read like a corporate brochure are almost always written rather than
spoken.

## The golden rule: "you", not "we"

The cheapest text improvement in the world. Compare:

> Our company offers complete interior fitting services, using the highest quality materials and an
> experienced team.

with

> I fit out your flat from bare walls: plastering, wiring, finishes. You get a quote itemised by
> materials and labour, so you can see where the money goes.

The second is not more talented. It just talks about the customer instead of the firm, and says one
concrete thing instead of three adjectives.

Take any paragraph of yours, count how often "we/our" appears and how often "you/your". If the first
number is bigger, rewrite it.

## What goes on each page

### The home page

Four things, in this order:

1. **What you do and who for**, in one sentence your grandmother would understand. Not a slogan.
   "I fit out flats in the city" beats "We transform spaces and create experiences".
2. **Three concrete reasons** somebody picks you. Concrete: "I work with my own crew, I do not
   subcontract", not "quality and professionalism".
3. **Proof** that you exist and deliver: work, numbers, years, real reviews. Whatever you have. If you
   do not have it yet, do not invent it — write less.
4. **One clear next step.** One button, not five.

### The service page

One for each main thing you sell. Each needs:

- What it includes, as a list. What it does **not** include, also as a list — that makes you look more
  serious, not weaker.
- What it costs, or at least where it starts. If you cannot give a price, give a story: "a two-bedroom
  flat, complete, came to between X and Y last year, depending on the finishes."
- How long it takes.
- How it goes, in three or four steps.
- Who it is **not** right for. You filter out the wrong customers before you both waste time.

### The "About" page

It is the most-read page after the home page, and the worst-written page on the internet.

Do not write the history of the company. Write why you are a safe choice: who you are, how long you
have been doing this, what you learned, how you work. One real photograph of you does more than three
paragraphs.

### The contact page

Every route to reach you, plus what happens after they send the message. "I reply the same working
day" lowers anxiety and raises the number of messages. More on why messages do not arrive in [the
article about forms](/en/blog/why-your-contact-form-gets-no-messages/).

## Five mistakes you can fix in five minutes

**"Welcome to our website!"** Delete it. It occupies the most valuable space on the page in order to
say nothing.

**Headings that say nothing.** "Our services" → "What I build, and what it costs". Headings are read
five times more often than the text.

**Ten-line paragraphs.** On a phone, a ten-line paragraph is a wall. Cut to three or four.

**"Contact us for details" instead of a price.** It is the sentence that kills the most enquiries. If
you genuinely cannot give a price, say why, and give a range.

**Superlatives with no evidence.** "The best", "market leaders", "innovative solutions". Nobody
believes them and they occupy the space where a fact would have fitted.

## How much do you actually have to write

Less than you think. For a business site: a home page, two or three service pages, an about page, a
contact page. Around 1,500 to 2,000 words in total. That is a day's work with the method above, or a
month if you wait for inspiration.

Write it badly and completely, then correct it. A mediocre text that is published earns more than a
perfect one that does not exist.
