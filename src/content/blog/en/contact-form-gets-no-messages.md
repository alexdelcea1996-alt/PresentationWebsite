There are two explanations for "I have a form but nobody writes to me", and they are completely
different.

The first: messages are being sent and are not reaching you. The second: people arrive at the form
and do not fill it in.

Start with the first, because it takes five minutes to check and because it is more common than
anyone would guess.

## Part 1: check whether the form actually works

**The test.** Open the site on your phone, on mobile data, not on your own computer. Fill the form in
with real details and send it.

Then check, in order:

1. **Did a confirmation appear?** If the page says nothing after the button is pressed, plenty of
   visitors will press it again, or leave convinced it failed.
2. **Did the email arrive?** Check Spam too. If it is in there, that is a real problem: forms that
   send *from* the visitor's address, instead of sending from an address on your own domain with the
   visitor's address in Reply-To, land in Spam often.
3. **Did it arrive at the address you actually read every day?** I have seen forms delivering
   perfectly into a mailbox nobody had opened in two years.

Repeat the test once a month. Forms break on their own: a key expires, a provider changes a setting,
somebody updates a plugin. Nothing tells you.

**The confirmation that lies.** The ugliest version: the form shows "Message sent!" whether or not it
went. The visitor leaves reassured, you receive nothing, and nobody finds out. Push on this with
whoever built your site: the success message must appear *after* the sending service confirms, not at
the same moment the button is pressed.

## Part 2: the form works, but nobody fills it in

If the test passed, the problem is the form or what surrounds it.

### Too many fields

Every extra field costs you messages. For a first enquiry you need: who you are, how to reach you, and
what you want. Three fields.

Full address, company name, VAT number, "how did you hear about us" — all of that can wait for the
second message, when the person is already involved. Whatever you demand before offering anything gets
paid for in lost enquiries.

### Required fields that should not be

A required phone number costs messages. Some people do not want to be called, especially in the
evening or at first contact. Make it optional and say why: "phone (optional, if you would rather I
called)".

### Nowhere does it say what happens next

The person pressing "Send" does not know whether you reply in five minutes or in two weeks. That gap
makes them hesitate, or look elsewhere in parallel.

One sentence next to the button fixes it: "I reply within 24 hours, usually the same working day."
That is exactly what this site says under its own send button, and it is not a marketing phrase — it
is a promise you can hold me to.

### The form sits at the end of a long road

If the contact button is only in the menu and the footer, and there are ten sections between the top
of the page and the form, you lose people on the way. Put a route to contact at the end of every
section that persuades: after the prices, after the work, after the FAQ.

### It is the only way to reach you

Not everybody fills in forms. Some prefer WhatsApp, some call, some email directly. Show all of them.
It does not fragment your enquiries — it increases them.

### It is unpleasant to fill in on a phone

Check on your own phone, not in a simulator: does the right keyboard appear for each field (numeric
for the phone, with an `@` for email)? Are the fields big enough to hit with a thumb? Does the button
stay visible when the keyboard comes up?

### It does not inspire confidence

A form with nothing around it — no name, no phone number, no address, no photograph — looks like a
postbox in a field. Put at least a real name and a real phone number beside it. And a link to the
privacy policy: it is both a legal requirement and a sign that you take things seriously. More in
[GDPR for a small website](/en/blog/gdpr-for-a-small-website/).

## What to try, in order

1. Test the form from your phone, on mobile data. Today.
2. Cut the fields you do not need for a first message.
3. Write under the button how quickly you reply.
4. Add WhatsApp and a phone number, visibly.
5. Put a route to contact at the end of the sections that matter.

The first three take an hour, and in my experience they are the ones that move the most.
