I get asked this a lot, and it usually gets an answer from someone with an interest in the outcome.
Let me try it the other way round: there are situations where you really do not need a website, and
they are worth knowing before you spend anything.

## When you genuinely do not need one

**You sell entirely through conversation and already have more demand than capacity.** A tattoo
artist booked three months out does not need a website to grow. They need more hands.

**You are testing an idea.** If you do not yet know whether the product sells, a social page and a
small advertising budget will answer that faster and cheaper than any website.

**You are effectively a single product sold through a marketplace.** If everything goes through
Amazon or Etsy, your energy is better spent there.

In every other case the question is not "website or social media" — it is what social media cannot
do for you.

## Four things a page will not do

### 1. It does not show up when somebody searches for what you sell

The person typing "emergency plumber, east side" into Google is not looking on Facebook. They look at
the first few results and they call. A Facebook page turns up there rarely and by accident; a page
built for exactly that search turns up because it was built for it. That is what [why nobody finds
you on Google](/en/blog/why-nobody-finds-you-on-google/) is about.

### 2. It is not yours

Your account exists for as long as the platform is happy. Accounts get suspended by mistake, and
getting one back runs through a form and an open-ended wait. This is not a conspiracy theory, it is
simply how a free account on somebody else's platform works.

A site on your own domain, with the content in your hands, cannot be suspended by anyone. [Who
actually owns your website](/en/blog/who-actually-owns-your-website/) applies here too.

### 3. It does not answer the questions that block a purchase

How much. How long. Do you deliver to my area. Can I have an invoice. What if I do not like it. In a
feed, all of that is buried under twenty posts with photographs. On a site it is one page somebody
reads at 22:40 while you are asleep, and decides without asking you.

That is the difference that shows up in practice: you stop spending your day answering the same three
questions ten times over.

### 4. It does not make you look like a company

Unfair, but real: for an order worth a few thousand, plenty of people check whether you exist. A web
address on an invoice, on a van or on a business card changes how you are read. Not because the site
is pretty, but because it means somebody invested in this business.

## What works best: both, with different jobs

It is not a choice. It is a division of labour.

**Social media brings people in and keeps them warm.** That is where you post work, show how you
work, answer comments. Rhythm, personality, living proof.

**The site closes.** That is where the person who has decided to find out the price and how to reach
you ends up. It is quiet, it is complete, it is yours, and it is there on the day the platform is
having a bad one.

The simplest test: look at your direct messages from the last month. If you keep answering "how much
is it?" and "do you cover my area?", you already have the evidence that a page is missing — one that
answers in your place.

## You do not necessarily need a big site

This is where people get lost: they assume the alternative to a Facebook page is a ten-page site with
a blog and a newsletter. It is not.

For a lot of businesses, one page done properly — what you do, who for, what it costs, how to reach
you — does ninety percent of the job. The difference between that and a full site is set out in
[landing page or business website](/en/blog/landing-page-or-business-website/).

And there is one more option people skip entirely: your [Google Business
Profile](/en/blog/google-business-profile/). It is free, it takes an afternoon, and for a local
business it often brings in more calls in the first year than the website does.

## The order I would suggest

1. Google Business Profile, filled in properly. Free.
2. One page that answers the money and time questions.
3. Social media, at a pace you can hold without burning out.
4. The rest of the site, when you need it.
