"I have a website but I do not show up on Google" is three different problems wearing the same name.
Each has a different answer, and starting with the wrong one costs you months.

Check them in the order below. The first takes two minutes.

## Step 1: are you even in the index?

Type this into Google, exactly like this, with your own address:

```
site:your-address.com
```

**If nothing comes back**, Google does not know you exist. That is not an "optimisation" problem, it
is an installation problem, and it usually has one of four causes:

- The site is new. Indexing takes anywhere from a few days to a few weeks. Sometimes you just wait.
- The "discourage search engines" option is still switched on, set during the build and forgotten.
  This is the number one cause on WordPress sites.
- A `robots.txt` file is blocking access.
- The site builds its content in the browser with JavaScript, and none of it exists in the page that
  gets delivered.

**What to do:** create an account on [Google Search
Console](https://search.google.com/search-console), verify the site is yours, submit the sitemap
(`sitemap.xml`) and request indexing for the home page. It is free, and it is the only place Google
tells you directly what it does not like.

## Step 2: you are indexed — but for what words?

If `site:` shows your pages, you are indexed. The next question is what you show up *for*, and this
is where the most common mistake lives.

Open Search Console → Search results → Queries. You get the real list of searches you were shown for.

Most of the time it looks like this: your company name, variations of your company name, and that is
about it. Meaning the people who already knew about you can find you. Everyone else cannot.

The cause is nearly always the same: your pages are written in your language, not your customer's.
The titles say "Services", "About us", "Portfolio". Nobody searches for "services". People search for
"floor fitting cost near me" and "office cleaning company city centre".

**What to do:** take the three or four main things you sell and give each one its own page, with a
title written the way somebody would search. Not a section inside a shared page — a page. Google
ranks pages, not companies.

To find out what is actually searched, do not guess: type the start of the question into Google and
look at the suggestions, then at "Related searches" at the bottom. Free, and closer to reality than
intuition.

## Step 3: you show up, but on page three

Now you are in a competition, and the answers get slow. Three things matter, in this order:

**The page genuinely answers the question.** If somebody is searching for a price and your page has
no price and no range, you are not the best answer, however well the rest is written.

**The site is usable on a phone, and fast.** Google measures this. It is not the main factor, but it
separates equals — and your visitor leaves anyway if the page drags. See [why your site loads
slowly](/en/blog/why-your-site-is-slow/).

**Somebody else mentions you.** A link from a partner, a supplier, a local article, a trade
association. Do not buy links. Ask for them where the relationship already exists.

## What does not help, though it is often sold

**Repeated keywords.** "Plumber London, best plumber London, emergency plumber London" does not lift
you. It is a pattern Google has recognised for over a decade.

**Meta keywords.** Not used for many years.

**Submitting to a hundred directories.** The two or three that matter in your trade, yes. The rest,
no.

**A blog written for Google.** Ten articles with no readers rank nothing. Three articles answering the
questions customers ask you on the phone, yes.

## What I would do in the first month, in order

1. **Search Console**, account created and sitemap submitted. One hour.
2. **[Google Business Profile](/en/blog/google-business-profile/)**, filled in. For a local business
   this brings in calls faster than anything else on the list.
3. **One page per main service**, with a title written like a search, and prices or ranges on it.
4. **Check mobile speed** with [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev).
5. **Ask for three links** from people who already know you.

None of that costs money. It costs a day, maybe two.

## One thing about patience

SEO has no button. Today's changes show up in weeks, sometimes months. Anyone promising you the top
spot within a month is either lying or talking about paid advertising — which is a different thing,
is perfectly legitimate, and is recognisable by the word "Sponsored".
