The site is finished, the invoice is paid, and three months later a subscription appears that nobody
mentioned at the start. It is not necessarily a swindle — often it is a conversation that simply
never happened.

Let us have it now, before you sign anything.

## Unavoidable, whatever the site is built on

**The domain.** Paid yearly. It is the one cost you genuinely cannot avoid, and the most important one
to keep in your own name — see [who actually owns your website](/en/blog/who-actually-owns-your-website/).

Put a calendar reminder a month before it expires. Domains lost through inattention are more common
than they should be, and getting one back costs far more than renewing it.

**Hosting.** Here the sums run from zero to a lot, depending on what you have:

- A static site (pages built in advance) can sit free on serious infrastructure with servers around
  the world. That is not a promotional offer, it is how this service is priced today.
- A WordPress site needs a server running a program and a database. That is a monthly cost, and the
  cheapest plan is often the cause of the problems in [why your site loads
  slowly](/en/blog/why-your-site-is-slow/).
- A shop with real traffic needs a serious plan. Do not economise here.

## Depends on what was built

**The SSL certificate** (the padlock in the address bar). Free today from almost any decent provider,
via Let's Encrypt, and it renews itself. If somebody bills you separately for it as an annual service,
ask why.

**Updates.** A WordPress site needs monthly maintenance: core, theme, plugins. Somebody has to log in,
update, and check nothing broke. Either you or somebody paid. A static site has no mandatory updates
because nothing runs on the server. The full argument is in [WordPress or a custom-built
site](/en/blog/wordpress-or-a-custom-built-site/).

**Backups.** Some hosting includes them. If not, it is a small cost and it is worth it.

**Paid plugins.** Advanced forms, bookings, SEO, security. Each with its own annual licence. Make the
list *before* launch, not after.

**Email on your own domain.** A few pounds a month per address from any serious provider. Worth it: an
address at your own domain changes how you are read.

## What gets sold as unavoidable, but is not

**A "maintenance subscription" for a site with nothing to maintain.** If the site is static and you
publish nothing, there is no monthly work. A subscription is legitimate only if it comes with a list
of things done each month — and only if you get a report.

**"Monthly SEO" with no deliverables.** SEO is real work, but you have to see *what* was done: new
pages, rewritten text, technical problems fixed. If the monthly report is only ranking graphs, you are
paying for a subscription to Search Console, which you have for free.

**"Security" as a separate service on a static site.** It has no database and runs no code. What
exactly is being secured?

**Small changes on a subscription when you make two a year.** Ask for an hourly rate. A monthly
retainer only makes sense if you use it.

## What an honest set of sums looks like

For a static business site, what you pay yearly is the domain and possibly the email. The rest can be
zero. That is not a promotion, it is a consequence of how it is built.

For a WordPress site, budget for hosting, monthly maintenance and licences. It is a real, predictable
cost and it belongs in the plan from the start.

For a shop, add the payment processor's fee on every transaction. Those are not website costs, but
they are costs.

## Three questions before you sign

1. **What is the total cost in year one, and in year two?** Year two is the revealing one: that is
   where the recurring items show.
2. **What happens if I stop the subscription?** Does the site stay online? The correct answer is yes.
3. **What exactly do I get each month?** If the answer is not a list, it is not a service.
