## Why sending ad traffic to your home page is a waste

You pay for a click. The person lands on your home page, where they find a menu with eight items, a
general description of the company and three buttons pointing in different directions. They came
for the ad they saw and can no longer find anything about it. They leave.

That is not their fault, and it is not the site's — a business website has a different job. The
problem is that the ad promised one specific thing and the page does not continue it. A landing page
is simply the page that continues the promise: same message, same offer, one next step.

That is also why it has no menu. Every link in a menu is an exit from a page you have just paid to
get somebody onto.

## How working together goes

We talk for 30 minutes about what you are launching, who it is for and where the traffic comes from.
You get a written quote with a fixed price and a deadline. I send you the structure of the page —
which sections, in which order, carrying which message — before I draw anything, because that is
where it is decided whether it works. Then I build it and give you a test link.

It usually takes about a week, and if you have a fixed launch date you tell me at the start and we
work back from it.

Once the campaign is live comes the part that matters most: the first 30 days, when we see what is
not working and change it. It is not a formality in the contract — a campaign page settles after
people see it, not before.
