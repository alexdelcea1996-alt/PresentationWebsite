## Signs you have outgrown spreadsheets

It is not about company size but about how much manual work surrounds your data. If two or three of
these sound familiar, you have probably crossed the line.

**One person owns the file** and something stalls whenever they are on holiday. The knowledge lives
in one head and one file, not in a system.

**The same data is typed in two places.** Somebody copies from a form into a spreadsheet, from the
spreadsheet into the invoicing software, from invoicing into the monthly report. Every copy is an
opportunity for an error.

**Approvals happen over chat.** It works until somebody asks who approved a discount three months ago
and nobody can find the message.

**The month-end report takes a full day.** That time is a tax you pay every month, indefinitely.

## What others do not tell you about the cost

The expensive part is not the programming, it is the decisions made wrongly at the start. A poorly
designed data structure gets paid for years later, when a simple feature suddenly requires a rewrite.

That is why I begin with analysis rather than code, and why I deliver in stages. I want you to hit
reality early, while changing direction is still cheap. A project that runs six months without anyone
using it is the surest way to build something you did not need.

And to be clear: if at our first conversation I work out that a product already on the market does
what you need, I will tell you which one. A €30-a-month subscription to something ready-made beats a
from-scratch application almost every time.

## How it goes

We start with an analysis stage where I document the process, the exceptions and the integrations
needed. You get a document and a fixed price for the build. We build the first usable version and you
put it into service. We add the rest based on what you learned using it. At the end you keep the
application, the code, the documentation and trained people.
