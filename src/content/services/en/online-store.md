## Why many small online stores do not sell

Not because of the product. Because of three things that repeat across nearly every store I look at.

**Shipping appears as a surprise at the end.** The customer adds an item believing they will pay €24
and discovers at the last step that it is €29. They abandon the cart, not over the €5, but because it
feels like a trick. Delivery cost has to be visible early, even approximately.

**Checkout demands an account.** Every extra field between cart and payment cuts orders. Nobody wants
to create an account to buy one pair of gloves once.

**The product page does not answer questions.** Sizing, delivery time, what happens if it does not
fit, who pays for the return. If the answers are not on the page, the customer does not write to ask
— they leave for someone who wrote them down.

## Marketplace or your own store?

The honest answer is that it is often both.

A marketplace gives you traffic from day one and is the right place to find out whether the product
sells. What it does not give you is margin — commissions frequently reach 15–25% — or the customer:
you do not have their details, you cannot build an email list, and the second order may go to a
competitor displayed right next to you.

Your own store works the other way round: you bring the traffic, but you keep the margin and the
relationship. It usually makes sense once you have steady marketplace sales and can feel the
commission.

If you are still in the testing phase, I will say so at our first conversation and let you sell on a
marketplace for another season. A store built too early is a cost sitting idle.
